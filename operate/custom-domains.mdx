---
title: Publish an Application on a custom domain
description: Verify your domain in Admin, publish a hostname, and route HTTPS requests to an Application.
icon: globe
---

You need access to manage domains for an organization with an active instance, access to the DNS zone, and permission to publish the target Application. Use the hostname and DNS instructions shown in Admin for your publication.

## Verify domain ownership

1. Open **Admin → Domains → Ownership** and select **Add domain**. Enter the registered domain, such as `example.com`. Verifying it covers eligible subdomains, such as `api.example.com`.
2. Open **Verification**. Create a TXT record in your DNS provider. If the provider automatically appends the zone, copy the relative **TXT host** into its Host or Subdomain field. Use the full name only when the provider asks for it. For the `example.com` zone, the relative host is `_revo-challenge` and the full name is `_revo-challenge.example.com`.
3. Copy the complete **TXT record value** from RevoEngine. After DNS publishes the record, use **Verify** and wait for **Verified**. If you're waiting for propagation, the next manual check is available after 15 minutes.
4. Keep the TXT record in DNS. RevoEngine checks it periodically. If you receive a warning about a missing record, restore the same value by the deadline shown in the notice.

## Publish a hostname

1. Open **Custom domains** and choose **Publish hostname**. Enter the hostname you want to use.
2. Follow **DNS records** for the hostname. Copy the displayed names and targets. If Admin reports a CAA restriction, ask your DNS administrator to allow `pki.goog` using the record shown in Admin. Keep existing issuer records that other services need.
3. Configure **Routes** with the public path, instance, and target Application. Use the root path `/` to forward requests without stripping a path prefix, and leave the target prefix empty to preserve the incoming path.
4. Wait for the hostname to become ready, then test it over HTTPS with a representative route and the expected credentials. Keep the required DNS records in place while the hostname is published.

If publication stays pending, check the TXT host, hostname DNS records, and certificate issuer policy in **DNS records**. You can verify ownership independently of hostname publication.

When retiring a hostname, remove its publication in **Custom domains** and then remove the DNS records dedicated to that publication. Review the organization's remaining hostnames before revoking ownership.
